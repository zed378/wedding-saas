# 00 - Design Direction

## Visual Direction of the Product (the App, Not the Template)
The application (dashboard, editor, admin) takes a **clean, modern, low-distraction** direction — because the emotional/decorative content lives in the INVITATION TEMPLATE itself (which varies), not in the app's chrome. The app must feel neutral & functional so it doesn't "compete" visually with the template preview being edited.

## Principles
1. **Preview-first**: wherever possible, show a real preview (template, photo) instead of a generic placeholder.
2. **Trustworthy**: since payments & personal data are involved, the UI must feel secure & professional (not overly playful) — the app's color palette is neutral (not the template's colors).
3. **Mobile-first, but the Editor is desktop-optimized**: the public page & marketing are mobile-first; the Editor itself is most comfortable on desktop/tablet (editing many fields), but must still be functional on mobile (WOs often work from their phone).
4. **Progressive disclosure**: don't show every option at once — an editor form per section, not one giant form.

## Tone of Voice (app copy)
- Casual-professional Indonesian, not stiff/formal, but also not overly casual/slangy.
- Clear, actionable instructions ("Add your first photo to start the gallery" instead of "Gallery is empty").

## Competitor Reference (for research, not to be copied outright)
Similar products in the Indonesian & regional market can be used as a UX benchmark (without copying their visual identity) — competitor research is documented separately by the design team before high-fidelity mockups begin.

## Design Deliverables
- The design system (06-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md) as the component source of truth.
- Low-fi wireframes for all main flows before high-fi.
- An interactive prototype for the Editor (highest complexity) before development starts.
